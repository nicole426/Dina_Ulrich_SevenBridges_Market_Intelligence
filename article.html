Follow the money

Palm Beach County ranks first among all U.S. counties for net incoming income, with roughly $23 billion in net domestic income gained from 2019 through 2023, according to a Miami Realtors analysis of IRS migration data. And the buyers arriving are not stretching: through August, South Florida million-dollar sales were up 21.7 percent year over year — nearly three times the growth of the overall market — with 60 percent of those purchases paid in cash, per Miami Realtors. Palm Beach County alone accounted for 30 percent of the region’s million-dollar transactions.

At the very top, the county broke its full-year record for $10 million-plus home sales in September — with more than three months of 2026 still on the clock, per Miami Realtors.

“The wealth is here. The question buyers are really asking is which address gives them certainty.”

The condo reset is a referendum on predictability

Florida’s post-Surfside reserve law is now fully in force. Condo associations in buildings three stories and up had to complete structural-integrity reserve studies by December 31, 2025, and owners generally can no longer vote to waive the reserves that fund roofs, structure, waterproofing and other big-ticket components (Section 718.112, Florida Statutes). The result is honest pricing of deferred maintenance — and, in older buildings, special assessments that arrive as surprises.

To be fair, this is not a countywide condo crash. Palm Beach County’s condo median actually rose 5.26 percent year over year in August to $300,000, per Miami Realtors — the pain is building-by-building, concentrated in older towers. But the supply math tells you where confidence sits: 3.5 months of single-family inventory versus 6.7 months on the condo side. Buyers with options are choosing the asset class where the costs are known in advance.

That is exactly the trade a gated single-family community offers — whether it’s a country club or a resort-style community like Seven Bridges. The costs are disclosed up front and attached to equity and lifestyle, not to an engineering report you haven’t seen yet.

Two paths behind the gates

Luxury buyers moving to south Palm Beach County are choosing between two kinds of gated address.

The country club. Communities like St. Andrews, Boca West, Woodfield and Broken Sound pair the home with a club membership — golf, racquets, dining and social life — and charge for it through a joining contribution and annual dues.

The luxury gated community. Communities like Seven Bridges, The Bridges, Lotus and Boca Bridges are built around resort-style clubhouses rather than a country-club equity membership. In Seven Bridges, that means a clubhouse of about 30,000 square feet with a restaurant, resort and lap pools, a fitness center, tennis and pickleball, as described in current listings there. Buyers get the gate, the amenities and the newer single-family construction — with no shared structure, no condo reserve study and no building-wide special assessment.

Both paths are winning for the same reason: certainty. Here is how the four gated communities I work most closely with stand right now:

The price of admission keeps rising at the clubs

On the country-club side, the pattern is the most telling thing in the market. Within roughly thirteen months, nearly every major Boca Raton club raised its cost of entry, according to published 2026 membership guides: Broken Sound made its contributions non-refundable as of October 2025; Boca West’s initiation is reported to rise from $150,000 to $215,000 effective October 1, 2026; Woodfield’s full equity from $225,000 to $300,000 effective November 2, 2026; and St. Andrews Country Club’s joining contribution moved to $500,000 — $550,000 all-in with the property owners association — effective September 1, 2026. Clubs do not publish fee schedules publicly, so verify current figures with the club directly before contracting.

“Boards do not vote for increases like these into weak demand.”

The clubs are also spending like they intend to keep winning: Broken Sound cites a $40 million clubhouse renovation and a $7 million spa, Woodfield a $7.9 million golf course renovation, and St. Andrews completed a $27.5 million enhancement program. A GGA Partners/CMAA study of club leaders found cost concerns concentrated among older members — 58 percent versus 17 percent for younger ones — while younger members engage most with wellness, racquets and social programming. In Boca, the luxury buyer now averages about 45 years old, per Behind the Hedges.

That last point matters for the gated communities too. The same younger buyer who wants wellness, racquets and a social calendar can find much of it at a Seven Bridges, Bridges, Lotus or Boca Bridges clubhouse — without a country-club joining contribution. That is why I see these communities as part of the same “club is the new address” story, not a separate one.

What this means in Seven Bridges

Seven Bridges has 15 homes on the market against 27 sales so far this year — about 0.56 listings for every sale, which puts it among the less-tight communities in the comparison below. The Bridges (11 active, 29 sold) and Lotus (13 active, 41 sold) are moving faster. Prices on the market in Seven Bridges today run from $1.48 million to $3.95 million.

For sellers, that means competition — pricing and presentation matter, and the homes that are priced to the market are the ones that move. For buyers, it means real choice across floor plans and price points, in a community whose amenities come with the address.

One honest caveat: no gated community, club or otherwise, is cost-free. Club ownership swaps the condo world’s surprise assessments for large disclosed contributions, annual dues and the possibility of future increases; a gated community has its own association dues and rules. The buyers winning in 2026 are not fleeing one cost for another — they are paying for certainty.

The address used to be the house. In 2026, it’s the community behind the gate.
